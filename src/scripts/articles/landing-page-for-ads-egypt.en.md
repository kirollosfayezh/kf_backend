You boosted a post or started a Google campaign. The dashboard shows hundreds of
clicks at a reasonable price. Then a week passes and the phone barely rings. The
usual conclusion is that ads "don't work for our business". More often, the ads
worked fine. The clicks landed somewhere that gave people no reason to stay.

## Why the homepage loses ad traffic

Your homepage is built for everybody. Someone who clicked an ad is different.
They saw one specific promise, such as a
discount on teeth whitening or a free site visit for a kitchen, and tapped to see
more of it. On a general page they have to search for it. On a phone, with a slow
connection and a thumb ready to go back, most of them will not. They leave, and
you pay for the click anyway.

## What a landing page actually is

A landing page is a single page built for one campaign. It has no full menu and
no distractions. It repeats the promise from the ad, explains it just enough, and
asks the visitor to do one thing.

It can live on your own domain, for example `yourcompany.com/offer`, which keeps
the tracking and the data under your control.

## What makes a landing page convert

### One offer

The headline should match the ad the person just clicked, in the same words if
possible. If the ad says "Free consultation this week", the page should say that
at the top, not "Welcome to our company".

### One action

Decide what a good result is before anything is designed: a WhatsApp message, a
call, a booking. Make that the obvious next step, repeat it after each section,
and remove everything that competes with it.

### A WhatsApp button and a short form

In Egypt, many people would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form.
Others prefer to leave a number and be called. Offer both:

- **A WhatsApp button** that opens a chat with a pre-filled message, so you know
  which campaign it came from
- **A short form**: name, phone, and at most one question that helps you qualify
  the lead

Every extra form field costs you some leads.

### Fast on a phone

Most ad clicks come from phones, often on mobile data. A slow page loses people
before they read the headline. Compress images, skip heavy sliders and
autoplay video, and test on a mid-range Android
phone, not only over office Wi-Fi.

### Proof

People are cautious with a business they found through an ad. Show them:

- Real photos of your work, your shop or your team
- Short reviews from real customers, with permission to use their names
- Prices or a price range, if you can share them
- Your address, working hours and how quickly you reply

Do not invent reviews or numbers. Visitors notice.

### Tracking

Install the **Meta Pixel** for Facebook and Instagram ads, and the **Google Ads
conversion tag** (or Google Tag Manager with GA4) for Google campaigns. Then define
what counts as a conversion: a form submitted, a WhatsApp or call button tapped.
Without this, you cannot tell a good campaign from a bad one.

## How to measure it

Clicks are not results. Track these instead:

1. **Conversion rate**: leads divided by visitors to the page.
2. **Cost per lead**: total ad spend divided by the number of leads.
3. **Lead quality**: how many leads became real conversations, and how many
   became customers. A simple sheet is enough.
4. **Source**: add UTM parameters to each ad link, so you know which campaign
   produced each lead.

Then change one thing at a time, or you will not know what worked.

These are typical market ranges, not a quote, and they vary a lot.

- **A single page on a template or page builder**: often somewhere around
  5,000 to 20,000 EGP, depending on design, copywriting and setup.
- **A custom-designed page** with full tracking, connected to a CRM or a sheet

- **Several pages for different campaigns**: priced by how many and how

## Common mistakes

- **Sending every ad to the homepage**
- **A headline that does not match the ad**, so visitors think they clicked the
  wrong link
- **Long forms** that ask for everything before any contact
- **No tracking**, or a Pixel installed but no conversion events defined
- **Leads that nobody answers**: a WhatsApp message left for a day is usually a
  lost customer, however good the page is
- **Judging too early**, on a few days of data

> A landing page does not make a weak offer strong. It makes a good offer
> easy to accept.

## Common questions

### Can I use my Facebook page instead of a landing page?

You can, and click-to-WhatsApp ads skip the website entirely. The trade-off is
less control over the design and less tracking data. A landing page on your own
domain gives you both.

### How long does it take to build a landing page?

If the offer, text and photos are ready, a single page can usually be built and
tested within one to two weeks. Waiting for content is the usual delay.

### Do I need a separate page for each ad?

Not for each ad, but usually for each offer or audience. Five ads for the same
discount can share one page. Corporate clients and home customers need separate
pages, because the words and the proof differ.

### How do I know if the problem is the ad or the page?

If the ad gets few clicks, the problem is the ad: the image, the text or the
audience. If it gets clicks but the page gets few leads, the problem is usually
the page or the offer. Tracking is what lets you tell them apart.
