Almost every restaurant in Egypt that delivers is on the delivery aggregator apps,
and for good reason: that is where hungry people open their phones. Sooner or later
the owner looks at the monthly statement, sees a commission on every order, and asks
whether it would be cheaper to have an app of their own.

Sometimes it is. Often it is not, at least not yet. Here is how to tell.

## What the aggregators actually give you

It is worth being fair to them first.

- **Reach.** Customers who have never heard of you find you.
- **Drivers.** Many restaurants use the platform's riders and never manage a fleet.
- **Payments and support.** Online payment, refunds and complaints are handled for
  you.
- **Zero build cost.** You upload a menu and start receiving orders.

## What they cost you

- **A commission on every order**, including orders from customers who already love
  you and would have ordered anyway.
- **The customer is theirs, not yours.** You usually see an order, not a person. No
  reliable phone list, no order history you can act on, no way to reach them next
  week.
- **You rent the relationship.** If the rules or fees change, you adapt.

The real cost is not the commission on a new customer. That is a fair marketing
fee. It is paying the same commission on the fiftieth order from the same regular.

## When your own channel makes sense

Your own ordering is worth building when at least a couple of these are true:

- **A large share of your orders are repeat customers.** They already know you and
  do not need to discover you.
- **You have several branches**, and routing orders to the right one is becoming a
  problem.
- **You want a loyalty programme** that actually knows who the customer is.
- **You already get phone and WhatsApp orders** and take them down by hand.
- **You have, or can hire, your own drivers** for at least some zones.

If you are a single new branch still building a name, stay on the aggregators. Your
own channel cannot create demand that is not there yet.

## What an own-ordering system needs

A menu page with a "call us" button is not an ordering system. A real one needs:

- **Menu with modifiers.** Sizes, extras, "no onions", combos and item availability
  that you can switch off when something runs out.
- **Branches and delivery zones.** The customer's address decides which branch gets
  the order, the delivery fee and the estimated time.
- **Order tracking.** Received, preparing, on the way, delivered, visible to the
  customer without them having to call.
- **Payment.** Cash on delivery is still expected in Egypt, so it must work
  smoothly, alongside online card and wallet payments.
- **Driver handling.** Assigning orders, collecting cash and seeing who is where.
- **Kitchen screen and POS integration.** Orders must land in the kitchen and the
  till automatically. If a cashier re-types every online order, you have built a
  new problem.
- **WhatsApp notifications.** Order confirmations and status updates on the app
  your customers already check.
- **An admin panel** so you can change prices, photos and hours yourself.

Whether your current till can accept outside orders is often the deciding factor;
see [ready-made or custom POS](/blog/ready-made-vs-custom-pos-restaurants).

## Website first, or app?

| | Ordering website | Mobile app |
|---|---|---|
| Customer effort | Opens from a link, no install | Must be downloaded |
| Works from WhatsApp, Instagram, a QR code | Directly | Needs a link to the store |
| Push notifications | Limited | Yes |
| Store accounts and review | Not needed | Needed |
| Best for | Starting out, casual orders | Loyal, frequent customers |

For most restaurants the answer is **web ordering first**: one link you can share on
every channel, no install. Add an app once you have regulars who order often enough
to keep it on their phone.

## A phased approach

1. **Web ordering** with menu, zones, cash and online payment, and orders going
   into your POS or kitchen screen.
2. **Customer accounts and history** so reordering takes seconds.
3. **Loyalty and offers** tied to real customer data.
4. **A mobile app** on the same backend, adding push notifications.
5. **Driver app and tracking** if you run your own fleet at scale.

## Moving customers over without leaving the aggregators

You do not have to choose one. Most restaurants should run both, and use the
aggregators for discovery and their own channel for regulars.

- **Put a card in every bag** with a QR code to your ordering site.
- **Offer something the apps cannot**: a loyalty reward, a direct-order discount or
  a dish that is only on your own menu.
- **Answer WhatsApp orders with a link** instead of taking them down by hand.
- **Keep prices sensible on both.** Customers notice, and punishing aggregator
  customers tends to backfire.

## What drives the cost

No honest figure exists before someone has seen your setup. What moves it: website only or website plus app; the number of branches and zones; how complex
your menu modifiers are; which payment methods you accept; whether you manage your
own drivers; and, above all, how your POS and kitchen are connected. An integration
with a till that has a documented API is a small job. A closed system can be the
largest line in the project.

## Common questions

### Will my own app replace the aggregators?

For most restaurants, no, and it should not try to. The aggregators are good at
bringing you customers who do not know you yet. Your own channel is for keeping the
ones who do, so running both side by side is the normal, sensible outcome.

### Do I need my own delivery drivers?

Not necessarily at the start. Some restaurants begin with pickup orders, or with
nearby zones a small team can cover, and expand delivery as volume grows.

### How do customers pay on my own site?

The same way they pay you today. Cash on delivery remains the default for many
customers in Egypt, so it must be built in properly, alongside online card and
wallet payments through a payment gateway for those who prefer to pay ahead.

### Is a website really enough to start with?

For most restaurants, yes. It works from WhatsApp, Instagram and a QR code on the
bag with nothing to install, and an app on the same system can follow once enough
regulars order every week.
