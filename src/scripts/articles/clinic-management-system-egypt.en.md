Most clinics in Egypt run on three things: a booking notebook at reception, the
clinic's WhatsApp, and the doctor's memory. It works until it doesn't. Two
patients turn up for the same slot. A returning patient's old prescription is in
a drawer somewhere. At the end of the month nobody is quite sure how much the
clinic made, or how much the insurance companies still owe.

A clinic management system replaces the notebook, the drawer and the guesswork
with one place everyone works from. Here is what it should actually do.

## What a clinic system should handle

At minimum:

- **Bookings** from the phone, WhatsApp and an online page, in one calendar
- **Reminders** to patients before their visit
- **A queue** at reception, so the doctor sees who is waiting and who is next
- **The patient file**: history, diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results and scans
- **Billing**: consultations, follow-ups, procedures and packages
- **Insurance and company contracts**, if the clinic works with them
- **Reports**: visits, revenue, no-shows and what each doctor did

Everything else depends on the specialty. A dental clinic needs a tooth chart. A
dermatology clinic needs before-and-after photos. A physiotherapy centre needs
session packages. A good system lets you add these without rebuilding the rest.

## Bookings and reminders

Patients book the way they already talk to you, and in Egypt that is mostly
WhatsApp and phone calls. The system should not force them onto something new.
It should put every booking into one calendar, whoever took it.

What makes the difference:

1. **No double booking.** Each doctor has slots and a visit length per visit
   type, so a 10-minute follow-up and a 40-minute procedure fit correctly.
2. **A reminder the day before**, by WhatsApp or SMS, with a simple way to
   confirm or cancel. A cancelled slot can go to the waiting list.
3. **An online booking page** linked from your Google Maps listing and social
   pages, showing only real free slots.

A WhatsApp assistant can take simple bookings and answer the usual questions
about prices, addresses and hours, as long as it hands over to a person for
anything medical. See
[WhatsApp chatbots for businesses in Egypt](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-for-business-egypt)
for what to automate and what not to.

## The patient file

The file is the part doctors care about most, and the part most systems get
wrong by making it slow to fill in.

It should show, on one screen, the patient's previous visits, chronic
conditions, allergies, current medicines and the last prescription. Writing a
new prescription should take seconds, with the medicines the doctor uses often
one click away, printed on the clinic's letterhead in Arabic or English.

Attachments matter too: lab results, X-rays and reports the patient brings on
their phone should be stored in the file, not in someone's WhatsApp gallery.

> If the doctor needs more time to type the visit than to see the patient, the
> system will be abandoned within a month.

## Billing, insurance and the daily close

Reception needs to know what to charge without asking: a new consultation, a
follow-up within the follow-up period, a procedure, or a session from a package
already paid for. The system should know the clinic's prices and rules.

If the clinic works with insurance companies or company contracts, each one has
its own prices, approvals and monthly claims. Tracking that on paper is where
money quietly goes missing. The system should record what each visit is owed by
whom, and produce the monthly claim.

At the end of each shift, a daily close shows cash, cards and wallets taken,
against what the system expected.

## Medical data is sensitive data

Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (Law 151 of 2020) treats health data as
sensitive. Whatever the law requires of your clinic in detail, a system holding
patient files should at least:

- **Limit who sees what.** Reception books and bills; it does not need to read
  diagnoses.
- **Log access and changes**, so you can see who opened or edited a file.
- **Back up automatically**, to a place that is not the reception computer.
- **Use proper logins**, one per person, never one shared password.

Ask any provider where the data is stored, who can access it, and how you get it
back if you leave.

## Ready-made or custom

A single doctor with one clinic is often well served by a good ready-made
system. It is cheaper to start and quick to set up.

Custom software starts to pay when the clinic has several doctors or branches,
specialty workflows a ready-made system handles badly, insurance arrangements
of its own, or a need to connect bookings to its website, app or lab. The
trade-offs are the same as in any business system; we covered them in
[custom software or off-the-shelf](/blog/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf-how-to-choose).

## Rolling it out without stopping the clinic

1. **Start with bookings and the patient file.** Billing and reports can follow.
2. **Bring the important history in.** At least chronic conditions and the last
   prescription for regular patients.
3. **Train reception first.** They use the system all day, and they will make or
   break it.
4. **Run the notebook alongside it for a week or two**, then stop.

## Common questions

### Should the system be online or installed on the clinic's computer?

An online system works from any device and every branch, and backups are
handled for you. An installed system keeps working when the internet drops.
Many clinics want both: online, with the ability to keep booking and billing
during an outage.

### Can patients book through an app?

Yes, but most patients will not install an app for a clinic they visit twice a
year. A booking page and WhatsApp reach more people. An app makes sense for
clinics with frequent visits, such as physiotherapy or dental treatment plans.

### Can we move our old records into the new system?

Usually, if they exist in Excel or another system. Paper files can be scanned
and attached to the patient, then entered gradually as patients come back.

### How long does it take?

A ready-made system can run within days. A custom clinic system typically takes
a few months for the first version, depending on specialties, branches and
integrations. Starting with bookings and patient files shortens that.
