A van sales app is a phone or tablet app your sales reps use on the road: it shows today's route, takes orders and payments at each shop, prints the invoice and tracks what is left in the van. For a distributor in Egypt, a useful one works without signal, knows each customer's credit limit and sends every sale back to your inventory and accounts by the end of the day. If it cannot do those three things, it is a digital order book, not a van sales system.

Most distributors start on paper: a route list, a carbon-copy invoice book and a notebook of who owes what. That works until you have more reps than you can phone in one evening.

## Route plans and visit lists

Each rep opens the app and sees today's customers in visit order, with address, map pin, phone number, open balance and last order. The supervisor builds the routes once, by area and day of the week, and adjusts them in the app instead of sending lists on WhatsApp every morning.

Details that matter:

- **An outcome for every stop.** Ordered, shop closed, no cash, no need, owner absent. A missed visit with a reason is information; a missed visit with no record is a gap.
- **Location at check-in.** The app records where the rep was when the visit was logged. It does not prove a sale, but it shows whether the shop was visited at all.

If you also want to track the vehicles themselves, with fuel and trips, that is a separate system; see our guide to [fleet tracking in Egypt](/blog/fleet-tracking-system-egypt).

## Orders that work offline

Signal drops in basements, on rural roads and inside large markets. The app must take a full order, apply prices and discounts and print the invoice with no connection, then upload everything when the signal returns. Ask to see it working in flight mode, not just a promise.

Two things decide whether offline mode is safe:

- **Invoice numbers.** Each device needs its own number series, so two reps working offline never issue the same invoice number.
- **Prices and limits on the device.** The rep's phone holds the current price list, promotions and each customer's credit limit, refreshed whenever it connects. Otherwise reps sell at last month's prices.

## Stock in the van

Treat each van as a small warehouse. In the morning the warehouse issues a loading list and the van's stock goes up. During the day every sale, sample and return moves it. In the evening the rep unloads, and the count is compared with what the app expected.

The app should stop a rep selling items the van does not carry (or turn them into a pre-order for later delivery), track batches and expiry dates if your products have them, and record damaged goods separately from returns. The end-of-day difference, per item and per rep, is the first number your manager will check. ## Collections, credit and returns

Most distributor losses are in money, not boxes. The app should:

- Show the customer's balance and overdue invoices before the rep takes a new order.
- Block or flag an order that takes a customer over the credit limit, with an override only a supervisor can approve.
- Record each payment against specific invoices, whether cash, cheque, wallet or bank transfer, and give the shop a receipt.
- Link every return to the original invoice, with a reason, so returns cannot be used to hide a cash shortage.
- Close the rep's cash at the end of the day: what was collected, what was handed over and the difference.

| On paper | In the app |
|---|---|
| Balance known only to the rep | Balance visible to the rep and the office before each order |
| Payments written in a notebook | Each payment tied to an invoice, with a receipt |
| Returns noted on the invoice copy | Returns linked to the original sale, with a reason |
| Van count done roughly | Expected and counted stock per item, every evening |

## Sync with inventory and the ERP

The app is only half the system. The other half is the back office: the warehouse, price lists, customer accounts and bookkeeping. Every invoice, payment and return from the road should arrive there once, automatically, without anyone re-typing it from paper.

If you already run an ERP or accounting system, the key question is whether it has an API or an import the app can use. If it does not, the van sales back office can hold customers, stock and balances itself and export to your accountant. Not sure you need a full ERP? Read [does a small business in Egypt actually need an ERP?](/blog/erp-for-small-business-egypt)

If your company issues e-invoices or e-receipts through the Egyptian Tax Authority's system, decide early whether sales from the road are submitted by the app, by the back office or by your existing ERP. Choose one path only, or the same sale is reported twice. This is general information, not tax advice; confirm the details with your accountant.

## Dashboards for the distributor

By the evening, the owner wants to know how much was sold, how much was collected and what is still outstanding. A useful dashboard shows:

- Sales and collections per rep, route and day, against target.
- Visit rate: planned stops compared with visited stops.
- Overdue balances by customer and by rep.
- Van stock differences, and returns by reason.

Every number must lead back to the invoices behind it. A total you cannot open is a total you cannot correct, a point we cover in [your dashboard is lying to you](/blog/your-dashboard-is-lying-to-you).

## Building or buying

Ready-made van sales apps exist and suit distributors whose process matches theirs. A custom build makes sense when your pricing, credit rules, returns or ERP do not fit, or when per-user fees add up across many reps. Whichever you choose, check:

1. Can it take a full order and print an invoice in flight mode?
2. Does each device have its own invoice number series?
3. Are credit limits enforced on the device, with a supervisor override?
4. Does the evening van count compare expected and counted stock per item?
5. How do invoices reach your inventory, your accounts and, if you use it, e-invoicing?

## Common questions

### Does the app need internet all day?

No. It should work fully offline during the day and sync whenever there is a signal. It does need a connection at least once a day to load prices, balances and the route.

### Can reps change prices?

Only within limits you set. A good setup allows a discount up to a set percentage and sends anything above it to a supervisor for approval.

### Can reps use their own phones?

They can, but company devices are easier to control: you can limit them to work apps and take them back when a rep leaves. Either way, test the app with the exact phones and printers your reps will carry.

### Can we start with orders only and add collections later?

Yes, but plan the data from the start. Customers, invoices and balances should live in one place, so adding collections later does not mean starting again.
