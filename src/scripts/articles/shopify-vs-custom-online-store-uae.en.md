For most new UAE shops with a standard catalogue, Shopify is the faster and cheaper way to start, and unlike Saudi Arabia and the rest of the GCC, the UAE has Shopify Payments. A custom online store is worth it when your Arabic storefront, pricing rules, B2B invoicing or links to your stock and accounting systems matter more than launch speed. Either way, the trade licence, TDRA approval and VAT invoices are your responsibility, not the platform's.

Last checked: 6 November 2026

This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. If you are weighing the same choice for Egypt, read [Shopify or a custom online store in Egypt](/blog/shopify-vs-custom-online-store-egypt), because the payment situation there is different.

## Shopify or custom at a glance

| | Shopify | Custom online store |
|---|---|---|
| Time to launch | Days to weeks with a ready theme | Weeks to months, depending on scope |
| Upfront cost | Low: a monthly plan, a theme and apps | Higher: design and development |
| Running cost | Monthly plan, app subscriptions, card and transaction fees | Hosting, maintenance and gateway fees |
| Card payments | Shopify Payments, or a third-party gateway with an extra Shopify fee | Any licensed gateway you contract with |
| Arabic storefront | Depends on the theme and on each app | Designed right-to-left from the start |
| Custom rules and integrations | Limited to what the platform and its apps allow | Built around your process |
| Code and data | You rent the platform | You own the code if the contract says so |

The rest of this guide explains the rows that usually decide the question in the UAE.

## Shopify Payments in the UAE: what it covers

Shopify lists the United Arab Emirates as a supported country for Shopify Payments. Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman are not on that list, so Shopify stores there have to use a third-party provider. For a UAE business, this removes the main reason other Gulf stores leave Shopify.

In the UAE, Shopify Payments lets you accept credit and debit cards and accelerated checkouts, and you activate it when you set up the store. Expect to verify personal and business information. Card processing rates depend on your Shopify plan, so compare them on Shopify's pricing page for the plan you will actually use, not the cheapest one.

The catch is the alternative. If you process payments through a third-party gateway instead of, or alongside, Shopify Payments, Shopify charges a third-party transaction fee on those transactions, on top of what the gateway charges. PayPal is exempt only when Shopify Payments is your primary provider. So a UAE store on Shopify has a strong financial reason to stay with Shopify Payments. If a payment method your customers want is only available through a local gateway, you pay two sets of fees for it.

Before you commit, check three things with Shopify directly:

- Whether your licence and entity type are accepted for Shopify Payments.
- The payout currency, and how long payouts take to reach your UAE bank account.
- Which payment methods your customers ask for, and whether Shopify Payments offers them.

## Your licence, TDRA approval and VAT come first

No platform choice changes the legal basics. Under Federal Decree-Law 14/2023, a business that sells online in the UAE needs a licence that includes e-commerce activity, plus approval from TDRA, and every transaction needs a clear digital invoice. Our guide to the [UAE e-commerce licence for an online store](/blog/uae-ecommerce-licence-online-store) covers the steps.

Where you are licensed matters too. Free-zone companies operate under a different legal framework, and to sell on the mainland they need a licensed mainland distributor or a mainland branch or company. Settle this with your licensing adviser before launch, because it decides which entity is the seller on your invoices and which bank account receives the money.

VAT in the UAE has a general rate of 5%. If you are VAT-registered, the store has to produce a correct invoice for every taxable order. On Shopify, check what the built-in order documents show and whether you need an app to produce VAT invoices in the format your accountant wants. In a custom store, the invoice layout, numbering and Arabic text are built to that format from the start.

E-invoicing is the other date to know if you sell to businesses. B2B and B2G invoices in the UAE move to e-invoicing through an Accredited Service Provider (ASP): from 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more, and from 1 July 2027 for businesses below that. Sales to consumers are excluded until the Minister decides otherwise. A store that sells only to consumers is not affected yet. A store with trade customers needs a plan for passing invoice data to its ASP, and a PDF or an email is not an e-invoice.

## Arabic storefront quality

A UAE store usually has to read well in both Arabic and English, and Arabic is where many online stores look careless:

- Mirrored layouts with arrows and icons pointing the wrong way.
- Prices, sizes and phone numbers broken up inside right-to-left text.
- A translated product page followed by a checkout that is half English.
- Filters, emails and invoices that still use the English labels.

On Shopify, Arabic quality depends on the theme and on every app you add, because each app brings its own text and layout. Test the whole path in Arabic before you pay for anything: home page, product page, cart, checkout, order confirmation email and invoice, on a phone. In a custom store, right-to-left is designed from the first sketch, and each language gets its own pages so both can appear in search.

## Payment gateways for a custom store

A custom store connects to a gateway you contract with directly. Names UAE businesses commonly meet include Network International, which is based in Dubai, Telr, PayTabs and Checkout.com. Each has its own fees, settlement times and onboarding checks, and each will ask for your trade licence. Two local rails are also worth asking about: Aani for instant payments and Jaywan, the domestic card scheme. Both are run by Al Etihad Payments, a subsidiary of the UAE Central Bank.

The gateway itself is rarely where custom stores fail. They fail on what happens around it:

1. Refunds and partial refunds that do not reach the order or the stock figure.
2. Payments confirmed by the gateway after the customer closed the browser, with no webhook to mark the order as paid.
3. Settlements that cannot be matched to orders, so the accountant reconciles by hand.
4. Cash-on-delivery orders that never get closed.

Ask for each of these in the scope before you sign. Our guide to [payment gateways in the UAE](/blog/payment-gateways-uae) compares the options, and [Building an online store in Egypt: payments, delivery and what actually breaks](/blog/online-store-egypt-payments-and-delivery) lists the failures we see in practice. Most of them apply in Dubai too.

## When a custom store is worth it

Shopify is usually the right start if:

- You sell physical products to consumers with a standard checkout and delivery.
- You want to launch quickly and test demand before spending on development.
- Card payments through Shopify Payments cover what your customers use.
- Your team will run the store without a developer.

A custom store is usually worth the cost if:

- Pricing follows rules that apps handle badly, such as trade prices per customer, quotes, minimum orders or bundles built to order.
- The store must share stock with your shop, warehouse or POS in real time.
- You sell to businesses and need invoices that fit your accounting and, from 2027, your e-invoicing set-up.
- Your customers want a payment method that Shopify would charge a third-party fee on, at a volume where that fee adds up.
- The Arabic experience is central to your brand and themes keep falling short.
- You want to own the code and the data, without depending on one platform.

There is also a middle path: start on Shopify, prove that people buy, then move to a custom build when the workarounds cost more than development would. If you plan this, keep your product data, customer list and order history exportable from the first day, and plan redirects from the old product URLs so you keep your search rankings.

## Comparing the cost honestly

Compare costs over three years, not at launch. For Shopify, add up the monthly plan, every paid app, the theme, card processing and any third-party transaction fees at the sales volume you expect. For a custom store, add up design and development, hosting, maintenance and gateway fees. Shopify usually wins in the first year. A custom store can win later if sales grow, if you pay for many apps, or if most of your payments would otherwise carry third-party fees.

Write the comparison down as a table with your own numbers, and include the time your team spends on workarounds. That hidden cost is often what tips the decision.

## Common questions

### Is Shopify Payments available in the UAE?

Yes. Shopify lists the UAE as a supported country, with card payments and accelerated checkouts. It is not available in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain or Oman, where Shopify stores use third-party providers.

### Can I use Telr, PayTabs or Network International with Shopify?

Ask the gateway whether it offers a Shopify integration for UAE merchants. If it does, Shopify charges a third-party transaction fee on those transactions in addition to the gateway's own fees, so compare the total with Shopify Payments.

### Do I need a licence before opening a Shopify store?

Yes. Selling online in the UAE needs a licence that includes e-commerce activity and TDRA approval, whichever platform you use. A free-zone company also needs a mainland distributor, branch or company to sell on the mainland.

### Does my online store need e-invoicing in 2027?

Not for sales to consumers, which are excluded for now. If you sell to businesses or government, those invoices move to e-invoicing through an ASP from 1 January 2027 or 1 July 2027, depending on your revenue.

### Can I move from Shopify to a custom store later?

Yes. Products, customers and orders can be exported and imported into the new store. Plan redirects from the old URLs and test payments and invoices before you switch.
