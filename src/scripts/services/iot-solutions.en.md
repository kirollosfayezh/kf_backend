The Internet of Things is simple in principle: a device measures something, sends it somewhere, and someone acts on it. Making that reliable — across hundreds of devices, weak connections and real users — is the hard part, and that is what we build.

We work across the whole chain: the device and its firmware, the cloud backend that collects the data, and the dashboards and apps people use.

## What we have built

- **[GenTrack](/projects/gentrack)**: live monitoring of 500+ rental generators across Egypt — location on a map, fuel anomaly detection, remote device management and a cloud ERP for the rental business, with custom hardware on each unit.
- **[Smart Balance](/projects/smart-balance)**: a Raspberry Pi scale that streams live weight readings to a mobile app.

## What an IoT system includes

- **Hardware and firmware**: sensors, controllers and the code that runs on them.
- **Connectivity**: cellular, Wi-Fi or local networks, with data buffered when the connection drops.
- **Cloud backend**: secure device registration, data storage and processing.
- **Dashboards**: live maps, charts and status for each device.
- **Alerts**: when a reading crosses a threshold, a device goes offline or behaviour looks wrong.
- **Remote management**: configure and control devices without a site visit.
- **Mobile apps** for field teams and customers.

## Where IoT pays off

- **Rental and fleet**: know where every asset is and how it is being used.
- **Energy and fuel**: catch theft and waste early.
- **Cold chain and storage**: temperature and humidity monitoring with alerts.
- **Facilities and factories**: machine status, counts and downtime.

## How an IoT project runs

Every IoT project goes through the same five stages, and it starts small:

1. **Discover.** We look at the assets you want to monitor, what you need to know about them, where they are and what connectivity is available on site.
2. **Plan.** We choose the sensors, the hardware for each unit, how it connects (cellular, Wi-Fi or a local network) and which alerts matter, and agree it in writing.
3. **Design.** We design the dashboard, the alerts and, if needed, the mobile app around the people who will act on the data.
4. **Build.** We start with a pilot on a few devices to prove the readings and alerts in real conditions. You see a working build every one to two weeks.
5. **Launch.** Once the pilot works, we roll out across the fleet, monitor the system and keep improving it.

## What an IoT project costs

Projects start from $200 (about SAR 750 / AED 735), and the final price depends on scope, with a fixed written quote after a free call. For IoT, these are the main things that drive the price:

| Cost driver | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Number of devices | More units means more hardware, more installation work and more data to handle. |
| Hardware | Custom hardware on each unit costs more than reading sensors you already have. |
| Connectivity | Cellular devices each need a SIM and a data plan; Wi-Fi or a local network may not. |
| Cloud running costs | Storage and processing grow with the number of devices and how often they report. |
| Software scope | A dashboard with alerts is smaller than a dashboard, a mobile app and an ERP. |

Hardware is a per-unit cost, and SIMs and cloud hosting are monthly costs on top of the software build, so the pilot also gives you real numbers for what a full rollout will cost to run. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do you supply the hardware?

We design and assemble the device setup for the project, or integrate with hardware you already have, depending on what the project needs.

### What happens when a device loses connection?

Devices store their readings and send them when the connection comes back, and the dashboard shows which devices are offline.

### Can we start with a few devices?

Yes. We usually start with a pilot on a small number of devices, prove the value, then roll out across the fleet.

### How long does an IoT project take?

It depends on the scope: how many devices, whether the hardware is custom, and what the software needs to do. We share a realistic timeline before we start, and the pilot on a few devices comes first, so you see real readings early.

### Where is the device data stored, and who owns it?

In a cloud account in your name, with the hosting provider and region you choose. The data, the source code and the accounts are yours.

### Can you take over or extend an existing IoT system?

Yes. We can audit the devices, firmware, backend and dashboards you already have, fix what is broken, then extend and maintain the system.
