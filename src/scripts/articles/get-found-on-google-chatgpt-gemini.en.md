Customers still search Google and Google Maps. More and more of them also ask an
assistant directly: "Who builds POS systems in Cairo?", "Which clinic system is
good in Egypt?", "Is this company real?" If the assistant has never come across
your business, or confuses it with another one that has a similar name, you are
not in the answer.

The good news is that the same work helps with both. Here is what actually
matters.

## Where assistants get their answers

Assistants answer from two sources: what they learned in training, and what
they find by searching the web at the moment you ask. For a business, the second
matters most, and each assistant searches differently:

- **Gemini** answers with Google Search.
- **ChatGPT** uses its own crawler and third-party search providers, of which
  Bing is the best known.
- **Claude** searches the web through a search provider; it is widely reported
  to be Brave Search.
- **Perplexity** runs its own index.

So the basics are the same as for search engines: your site has to be crawled
and indexed, it has to say clearly who you are and what you do, and other
trustworthy places on the web have to confirm it.

## Why assistants mix up businesses

An assistant looking for "KF Tech in Egypt" finds every business with a similar
name and tries to decide which one you mean. It gets it wrong when:

- Your name is written differently in different places
- Your social profiles do not link to your website
- Your website is new, or not yet indexed
- Little else on the web mentions you

Every step below reduces that confusion.

## Step 1: one fast, official website

Your website is the reference everything else should point to. It should be on
your own domain, load quickly on a phone, and work without relying on JavaScript
to show its text. Many crawlers, including some assistants' crawlers, read the
raw HTML and never run your scripts, so a page that only appears after
JavaScript loads can look empty to them.

Each service deserves its own page that says what you do, for whom, where, and
roughly what it costs. Our guide to
[what a company website costs in Egypt](/blog/company-website-cost-egypt) covers
what to expect when building one.

## Step 2: register with Google and Bing

1. **Google Search Console**: verify your site and submit your sitemap
   (usually `/sitemap.xml`). This is how Google, and therefore Gemini, learns
   about your pages, and it tells you which pages are indexed and which are not.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools**: do the same. Bing also accepts **IndexNow**, a
   simple way for your site to announce new or changed pages the moment they
   are published.

Both are free, and both take less than an hour.

## Step 3: the same identity everywhere

Use exactly the same business name, phone number and address on your website,
your Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram and any directory.
Put your website link in every profile. Our guide to the
[Google Maps business profile](/blog/google-maps-business-profile-egypt) walks
through the local listing step by step.

On the website itself, **structured data** (schema.org markup) states the facts
in a form machines read directly: your official name and the short forms people
type, your logo, contact details, and links to your official profiles.

> If people search for your business by a short name, say so in your
> structured data. Otherwise the short name belongs to whoever uses it more.

## Step 4: let the crawlers in

Check your `robots.txt`. Some sites block AI crawlers by accident, or because a
template did it by default. If you want to be found by assistants, allow their
search and user agents, such as `OAI-SearchBot` and `ChatGPT-User` for ChatGPT,
`Claude-SearchBot` and `Claude-User` for Claude, and `PerplexityBot`. Gemini
relies on Google's normal crawler.

Some sites also publish an `llms.txt` file: a plain summary of the business and
its key pages written for assistants. It is a young convention and no assistant
promises to use it, but it costs little and states your facts clearly.

## Step 5: content that answers real questions

Assistants quote pages that answer the question asked. Write the pages your
customers need before they buy:

- What the service includes, and what it does not
- Typical prices or what moves the price
- How long it takes, and how the work is done
- Real projects, with the problem and the result
- Frequently asked questions, in plain language

In Egypt, write in Arabic as well as English. Many customers search in Arabic,
often in Egyptian dialect, and an English-only site misses them.

## Step 6: be mentioned elsewhere

Search engines and assistants trust what other sources confirm. Over time, aim
for:

- **Reviews** on your Google Business Profile, from real customers
- **Listings** on reputable business and agency directories in your field
- **Mentions** by clients, partners, local media and events, with a link

There are no shortcuts here, and the fake ones backfire: bought reviews,
paid link schemes and pages stuffed with keywords can get a site demoted.

## How long it takes

A new site or a new domain usually takes weeks to be indexed properly and months
to build authority. Assistants follow the same curve, because they rely on the
same signals. Consistency matters more than speed: one accurate identity,
repeated everywhere, and useful pages added regularly.

## Common questions

### Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT's or Gemini's answers?

There is no way to buy a place in an assistant's own answer. It comes from what
the assistant can find and trust about you. That is why the steps above matter.

### I asked an assistant about my company and it named a different one. What now?

This usually means your name is shared with other businesses and your own site
is not yet well indexed or linked. Make the name consistent, link every profile
to your website, register in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and give
it time.

### Does telling ChatGPT the right answer fix it?

Only in your own conversations. Other people's answers come from what the
assistant finds on the web, so the fix has to happen there.

### Is SEO still worth it if people use assistants?

Yes. Assistants that search the web rely on the same indexes, and a site that
ranks well and explains itself clearly is far more likely to be quoted.
