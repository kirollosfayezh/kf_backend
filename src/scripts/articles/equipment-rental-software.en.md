Equipment rental software keeps one record per machine, whether it is a generator, a crane, a compressor or a set of scaffolding, and ties it to the contract it is on, where it is, how many hours it has run and when it is due for service. For a rental company in Egypt, it replaces the notebook, the WhatsApp group and the spreadsheet that never quite agree. The parts that matter most are contracts, utilisation, billing by hours or fuel, remote monitoring and maintenance.

## Why renting is harder than selling

Selling a machine is one transaction. Renting it out is a loop that repeats for years: quote, contract, delivery, use on site, return, inspection, service, and out again. Each turn of that loop is a chance to lose money:

- A machine came back but was never checked in, so it still shows as rented and nobody offers it.
- Hours ran past the contract and nobody billed them.
- Fuel was delivered to site but never reached the generator.
- A service was missed because the hour meter was read once a month, by hand.

Accounting and retail software track stock that leaves and does not come back. Rental needs an asset that leaves, comes back and earns again. That is the gap a rental system fills. ## Contracts: from quote to return

The contract is the centre of the system. A good one holds:

- **The customer, the site and the contact person** on site.
- **The machines on it**, by serial number, not just "two 100 kVA generators".
- **The rate and the billing basis**: daily, weekly, monthly, per engine hour, or a mix.
- **Delivery and pick-up**, with who drove, when, and the condition and meter reading at each end.
- **Deposits, extensions and early returns**, recorded as changes to the contract, not as a new contract typed from scratch.

When a customer extends by phone, staff should be able to extend the contract in a couple of clicks and keep the history. When a machine is swapped on site because it broke down, the contract should show both machines and the dates each one was there, so the bill comes out right.

## Utilisation: which machines earn

Utilisation is the share of time a machine is out on hire and earning. It is the number that tells you whether to buy more of a model, move units between branches, or sell the ones that sit in the yard.

Software can only give you this if every check-out and check-in is recorded. Once it is, the dashboard can show, for each machine and each model:

- Days on hire against days available in the period.
- Days lost to repair.
- Revenue earned against what the machine cost.

Even a simple monthly utilisation report changes buying decisions, because it replaces "we always seem short of 60 kVA units" with a number.

## Billing by day, by hour or by fuel

Rental companies bill in different ways, often within the same contract. The system should support the ones you use, not force one model on you.

| Billing basis | What the system needs | Where it goes wrong by hand |
|---|---|---|
| By day, week or month | Contract dates, extensions, early returns | Extensions agreed by phone and never billed |
| By engine hour | Meter readings at the start, the end and during the hire | Readings taken late, guessed or lost |
| By fuel | Fuel delivered, fuel level, consumption per hour | Fuel that left the tank while the engine was off |
| Minimum charge plus extra hours | Both of the above, with the agreed threshold | Extra hours missed once the minimum is covered |

Billing by hours or by fuel is only as accurate as the readings. When readings are entered by hand, the invoice depends on whoever wrote them down. When a device on the machine sends them, the invoice can be built from the data.

If your company issues invoices through the Egyptian Tax Authority's e-invoicing system, plan for the rental system to send them there rather than having staff retype them. A full [ERP](/blog/erp-for-small-business-egypt) is one route; for a smaller company, a rental system connected to its accounting is often enough.

## Remote monitoring: what the machine is doing now

For generators and other engine-driven equipment, a small device installed on each unit can report location, running status, engine hours and fuel level. Questions that used to need a phone call to site become a look at a map:

- Is the generator running, and since when?
- Is it still at the customer's site, or has it moved?
- Did the fuel level drop while the engine was off?

The last question is where monitoring earns its keep: a fuel drop with no engine hours to explain it can be flagged the same day, not found in the monthly fuel bill. The same logic applies to vehicles, which is why [fleet tracking systems](/blog/fleet-tracking-system-egypt) are built in a similar way.

Devices should store readings when the signal drops and send them when it returns, so a gap in coverage does not become a gap in billing.

## Maintenance driven by running hours

Engines are serviced by running hours, not by the calendar. When the system knows each machine's hours, it can:

- Warn when a unit is close to its next service interval.
- Stop a unit from going on a new contract while its service is overdue.
- Keep the service history per serial number: parts, oil, filters and who did the work.

## Common questions

### Can I start without monitoring devices?

Yes. Contracts, utilisation, billing and maintenance all work with readings entered by staff. Devices can be added later, machine by machine, starting with the units where fuel or hours matter most.

### Does it only work for generators?

No. The same structure fits construction equipment, cranes, compressors, scaffolding, event equipment and tools. Monitoring devices matter most for anything with an engine; for items without one, the system tracks contracts, location and condition.

### Can customers see their own rentals?

They can, if the system includes a customer portal or app. Customers then see the machines on hire, the running hours and their invoices, which cuts the calls asking for readings.

### How long does setup take?

It depends on the number of machines, the billing rules and whether hardware is installed. Loading the fleet and the open contracts usually comes first, so the system starts from the real position of every machine.
