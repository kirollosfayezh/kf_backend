If your UAE business had less than AED 50 million in revenue in its most recent accounting period, you must appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP) by 31 March 2027 and start sending your B2B and B2G invoices as e-invoices on 1 July 2027. Businesses at AED 50 million or more go live earlier, on 1 January 2027, and government entities on 1 October 2027. Below is how to tell which group you are in, what the rules cover, what changed in October 2026, what the penalties are and a 90-day plan to get ready.

Last checked: 6 November 2026

This article is general information, not tax or legal advice. The Ministry of Finance has amended these rules several times, so confirm your own position with your tax adviser and the Ministry's [eInvoicing page](https://mof.gov.ae/en/about-ministry/mof-initiatives/einvoicing/) before you act.

## Which group you fall into

Apart from government entities, which have their own dates, the UAE phases e-invoicing by revenue, and the line is AED 50 million. Ministerial Decision 244 of 2025 defines revenue as **gross income in the most recent accounting period**, so take the figure from your latest completed accounting period, not from a forecast or from this year's sales so far.

Work through these questions in order:

1. **Are you a government entity?** If yes, you follow the government timeline below.
2. **Was your gross income in the most recent accounting period AED 50 million or more?** If yes, you are in the first group, and your ASP deadline has already passed.
3. **Was it below AED 50 million?** Then you are in the SME group this checklist is written for.
4. **Do you issue invoices to other businesses or to government?** If yes, those invoices are in scope from your go-live date.
5. **Do you only sell to consumers?** Read the scope section carefully, because you may still be a buyer on your suppliers' e-invoices.

If your revenue sits close to the line, settle it with your tax adviser early. The two groups are six months apart, and finding out in December that you are in the January group leaves no time.

### The 2027 deadlines

The timeline comes from Ministerial Decision 244 of 2025 as amended. A pilot and voluntary phase began on 1 July 2026. That phase was not a mandate for everyone; the mandatory dates are in 2027.

| Group | Appoint an ASP by | Go live |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot and voluntary adopters | Not applicable | 1 July 2026 |
| Revenue of AED 50 million or more | 30 October 2026 | 1 January 2027 |
| Revenue below AED 50 million | 31 March 2027 | 1 July 2027 |
| Government entities | 31 March 2027 | 1 October 2027 |

For an SME, the two dates that matter are **31 March 2027**, when an ASP must be appointed, and **1 July 2027**, when invoices must start flowing through it. The gap between them is your testing window. Treat 31 March as the date your ASP contract is signed and your system is ready to connect, not the date you start looking.

## What the mandate covers

The scope is set out in the Ministry's announcement of the two decisions in September 2025:

- **B2B and B2G invoices are in scope.** Invoices you issue to other businesses or to government entities must become e-invoices, apart from specific exclusions such as the tax-group pause described below.
- **B2C is excluded for now.** Ministerial Decision 244 excludes business-to-consumer transactions "until such time determined by a decision issued by the Minister".
- **Both sides appoint an ASP.** The issuer and the recipient of an e-invoice each need one, because the invoice travels from the supplier's ASP to the buyer's ASP.
- **A PDF is not an e-invoice.** The Ministry states that PDFs, Word files, scans and emails do not count. The invoice must be structured data in the UAE's PINT AE format.

The model behind this is what the Ministry calls a "Decentralized Continuous Transaction Control and Exchange" with five corners. You (corner 1) send PINT AE data to your ASP (corner 2). Your ASP validates it and passes it to your buyer's ASP (corner 3), which delivers it to your buyer (corner 4). Your ASP also reports the tax data to corner 5, the tax side. Our guide to [UAE e-invoicing for custom POS and ERP systems](/blog/uae-e-invoicing-custom-pos-erp) walks through the corners from your system's point of view.

Onboarding, as the Ministry described it at an awareness event in September 2026, has three steps: choose an ASP, register on EmaraTax, then connect. The Ministry's list showed 86 accredited ASPs when we checked on 6 November 2026, so you are choosing from a long list, not waiting for one.

## What changed in October 2026

Ministerial Decision 197 of 2026, announced on 19 October 2026 and effective from 1 October 2026, made three changes that matter to SMEs:

- **Tax groups get a pause.** Transactions between members of the same tax group are excluded for 24 months from 1 January 2027.
- **Invoice data no longer has to be stored in the UAE.** The earlier requirement to keep it inside the country was removed.
- **No more system-failure notices to the FTA.** The duty to notify the Federal Tax Authority when your system fails was removed.

The first change helps groups of companies that invoice each other. The second widens your choice of software and hosting, although sector rules for payment or health data still apply. The third removes one reporting step, not the duty to issue and send your invoices.

## Penalties under Cabinet Decision 173/2026

On 30 October 2026 the Ministry announced updated administrative penalties in Cabinet Decision 173 of 2026, which replaced Cabinet Decision 106 of 2025. These are the amounts as announced on that date:

| Violation | Penalty | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Not implementing e-invoicing or not appointing an ASP | AED 5,000, then AED 5,000 per month | AED 50,000 in any 12 months |
| Each e-invoice or credit note not issued or not transmitted | AED 100 | AED 5,000 per month |
| Not telling your ASP about changed registration data | AED 250 per day | AED 5,000 per month |

Two points stand out for a small business. First, missing the ASP appointment carries a monthly penalty, so a late start costs more the longer it runs, up to AED 50,000 in any 12 months. Second, the per-invoice penalty also applies to **credit notes**, so returns and corrections need to go through the same route as sales invoices. If your current process issues credit notes by hand in a spreadsheet, that is the first thing to fix.

## A 90-day plan for SMEs

If you start in early November 2026, ninety days take you to early February 2027, which leaves about two months of margin before the 31 March ASP deadline. Run the plan in three blocks.

### Days 1 to 30: know what you issue

- Confirm your group with your adviser, using gross income from your most recent accounting period.
- List every place an invoice or credit note is created: POS, ERP, accounting software, spreadsheets, a salesperson's laptop.
- Split your invoices into B2B, B2G and B2C, and count how many of each you issue in a typical month.
- Check your customer records for complete business names, addresses and tax registration details, and note the gaps.
- Ask your software vendor, in writing, whether the system can export invoices as structured data and send them to an ASP.

### Days 31 to 60: choose your route and your ASP

- Decide how invoices will reach the ASP: typed into the ASP's own portal, uploaded in batches, or sent automatically from your POS or ERP through an API.
- Shortlist three ASPs from the Ministry's accredited list and ask each one the same questions: pricing per invoice or per month, how they connect to your system, how they handle credit notes and rejected invoices, and how you receive e-invoices from your suppliers.
- If your system cannot send structured data, decide whether to upgrade, replace or extend it. Our article on [custom software versus off-the-shelf](/blog/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf-how-to-choose) covers that decision.
- Appoint your ASP and keep a record of the date.

### Days 61 to 90: register, connect and test

- Register on EmaraTax as the Ministry's onboarding steps describe.
- Connect your system to the ASP and send test invoices that look like your real ones: discounts, several VAT lines, a credit note, a foreign customer.
- Agree who in your team watches for rejected invoices each day and who fixes them.
- Set up a routine for telling your ASP when your registration data changes, since that has its own daily penalty.
- Train the people who issue invoices, then run a full week of test invoicing.

From February to the end of June, fix what testing exposed, clean the rest of your customer data and ask your ASP how you will receive e-invoices from larger suppliers who go live in January.

## Common questions

### We only sell to consumers. Do we need to do anything?

Your B2C sales are excluded until the Minister decides otherwise. But you are still the buyer on your suppliers' invoices, and the Ministry says both the issuer and the recipient of an e-invoice must appoint an ASP. Ask your tax adviser how this applies to you, and check whether any of your sales are actually to businesses.

### Is a PDF invoice sent by email still enough?

Not for in-scope invoices after your go-live date. The Ministry states that PDFs, Word files, scans and emails are not e-invoices. The invoice must be structured PINT AE data sent through your ASP.

### Can we keep our current POS or accounting system?

Yes, if it can produce structured invoice data and send it to an ASP, or if your ASP offers a way to connect it. If it cannot, you can extend it, replace it or enter invoices in the ASP's portal. The right answer depends on how many invoices you issue each month.

### Do we have to store our invoice data inside the UAE?

No longer. Ministerial Decision 197 of 2026 removed the requirement to store e-invoice data in the UAE. Other rules can still apply to specific kinds of data, such as payment or health data.

### What if our revenue is just above or below AED 50 million?

Revenue here means gross income in your most recent accounting period. If you are near the line, confirm your group with your tax adviser now. Being in the larger group means your go-live date is 1 January 2027, not 1 July 2027.
