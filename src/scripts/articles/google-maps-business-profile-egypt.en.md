When someone in Nasr City searches "pharmacy near me" or "car service Maadi",
Google shows a map with a handful of businesses before any normal results.
Those few spots get the calls and the visits. Getting into them is not a trick.
It is mostly giving Google complete, consistent information and keeping it
current.

## How Google decides who shows up

Google says local results are based on three things:

- **Relevance**: how well your profile matches what the person searched for
- **Distance**: how far you are from the searcher, or from the area they named
- **Prominence**: how well known you are, from reviews, links, articles and your
  general presence on the web

You cannot change distance. Everything else in this guide is about relevance and
prominence.

## Setting up and verifying your profile

A Google Business Profile is free. You create it from Google Maps or the
Business Profile site with a Google account that the business controls, not a
staff member's personal account.

Google then asks you to verify that the business is real and that you run it.
The methods vary by business and change over time: a phone call or SMS, an
email, a postcard, or a short video showing the sign, the inside and proof that
you manage it. Use whatever Google offers, and do not edit the name or address
while verification is pending.

If you visit customers rather than receive them, such as a plumber or a mobile
car wash, set yourself up as a service-area business and hide the address.

## The details that decide relevance

### Name

Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your sign. Adding
keywords like "Best Dental Clinic Cairo" to the name is against Google's
guidelines and can get the profile suspended.

### Categories

The primary category is one of the strongest signals you control. Choose the
most specific one that describes what you are, such as "Dental clinic" rather
than "Medical clinic". Add a few secondary categories only if they are genuinely
true.

### Hours, phone and address

Keep hours accurate, including Ramadan and holidays. A customer who arrives at
a closed shop rarely comes back. Use one main number that someone answers.

### Photos

Add real photos: the storefront so people recognise it from the street, the
inside, your products or work, and your team. Stock photos do not help anyone
find your door.

### Products, services and posts

List your main services or products with short descriptions. Posts share
offers, news or events. They are not a ranking shortcut, but a current, active
profile persuades more people than an empty one.

## Reviews and replying to them

Reviews affect both prominence and whether people choose you. Ask every happy
customer for one, at the right moment: after the job is done, with a direct
link or a QR code at the counter.

What not to do:

- **Do not buy reviews** or post them from staff and family accounts. Google
  removes them, and repeated violations can restrict the profile.
- **Do not offer discounts in exchange for reviews.**
- **Do not ask only the customers you expect to be happy.** Google's policy
  does not allow selectively asking for positive reviews.

Reply to reviews, good and bad. For a complaint, reply calmly, acknowledge the
problem and offer to fix it privately. Future customers read your reply as
closely as the complaint.

## Why your website still matters

Google retired its free Business Profile websites in 2024, so the website link
on your profile should point to your own site. That site does two jobs.

**It supports ranking.** Prominence includes your presence on the web. A real
website with clear pages about your services and location gives Google more to
work with than a profile alone.

**It converts the click.** Someone who taps "Website" is comparing you with the
next result. If the page is slow on mobile data, has no Arabic, or hides the
phone number, they go back to the map.

What to get right:

- **The same name, address and phone number** as the profile, written the same
  way, in the footer or contact page
- **A page per branch** if you have more than one, each linked from its own
  profile
- **Fast loading on a phone**, with a tap-to-call button and a map
- **Structured data** that tells search engines your business type, address and
  hours
- **Arabic and English** if your customers search in both

## Common mistakes

- **The wrong primary category**, often a generic one, so you never show for
  the searches that matter
- **Inconsistent phone numbers**: one on the profile, another on the website,
  a third on Facebook
- **Fake or bought reviews**, which tend to be removed and damage trust
- **Duplicate profiles** for the same location, created by former staff or
  agencies
- **A profile owned by an agency or employee** instead of the business
- **Outdated hours and photos** after a move or renovation

## What to measure

The profile's Performance section shows how many people found you, and whether
they called, asked for directions or clicked through to your website. Check it
monthly and look at the trend, not single days.

Add tracking parameters to your website link so visits from the profile show up
separately in your analytics, along with what those visitors do next. The
numbers that matter are actions: calls, direction requests and enquiries. Views
alone tell you little.

## Common questions

### How long until my business appears on Google Maps?

After verification the profile usually appears within days. Ranking well for
competitive searches takes longer, and depends on your category, reviews,
website and how close you are to the searcher.

### Can I have one profile for several branches?

No. Each real location should have its own profile with its own address, phone
number and hours. You can manage all of them from one account.

### Can I remove a bad review?

You can report a review that breaks Google's policies, such as spam, abuse or a
review from someone who was never a customer. You cannot remove a review just
because it is negative.
