The Internet of Things is simple in principle: a device measures something, sends it somewhere, and someone acts on it. Making that reliable — across hundreds of devices, weak connections and real users — is the hard part, and that is what we build.

We work across the whole chain: the device and its firmware, the cloud backend that collects the data, and the dashboards and apps people use.

## What we have built

- **[GenTrack](/projects/gentrack)**: live monitoring of 500+ rental generators across Egypt — location on a map, fuel anomaly detection, remote device management and a cloud ERP for the rental business, with custom hardware on each unit.
- **[Smart Balance](/projects/smart-balance)**: a Raspberry Pi scale that streams live weight readings to a mobile app.

## What an IoT system includes

- **Hardware and firmware**: sensors, controllers and the code that runs on them.
- **Connectivity**: cellular, Wi-Fi or local networks, with data buffered when the connection drops.
- **Cloud backend**: secure device registration, data storage and processing.
- **Dashboards**: live maps, charts and status for each device.
- **Alerts**: when a reading crosses a threshold, a device goes offline or behaviour looks wrong.
- **Remote management**: configure and control devices without a site visit.
- **Mobile apps** for field teams and customers.

## Where IoT pays off

- **Rental and fleet**: know where every asset is and how it is being used.
- **Energy and fuel**: catch theft and waste early.
- **Cold chain and storage**: temperature and humidity monitoring with alerts.
- **Facilities and factories**: machine status, counts and downtime.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you supply the hardware?

We design and assemble the device setup for the project, or integrate with hardware you already have, depending on what the project needs.

### What happens when a device loses connection?

Devices store their readings and send them when the connection comes back, and the dashboard shows which devices are offline.

### Can we start with a few devices?

Yes. We usually start with a pilot on a small number of devices, prove the value, then roll out across the fleet.
