A salon or gym booking system in Saudi Arabia or the wider Gulf should do four jobs well: take bookings against real staff and room availability, track memberships and packages, send reminders on WhatsApp, and issue a correct VAT invoice for every payment. If a system does those four things, the rest is detail. If it misses one, your front desk goes back to a notebook and a phone.

Last checked: 6 November 2026

This guide walks through each job, what to ask before you choose, and when it makes sense to build your own system instead of subscribing to a ready-made one.

## Bookings and staff schedules

A booking is never just a time. It links a service, a staff member and a slot, and often a room or a chair. A 90-minute colour treatment with a specific stylist blocks that stylist and that chair. A personal training session blocks the trainer and part of the gym floor.

What to look for:

- **Services with real durations**, including setup and cleaning time between clients.
- **Staff working hours, breaks and days off**, so customers can only book times that actually exist.
- **Separate calendars for men's and women's sections**, if your branch runs them, with their own staff and hours.
- **A booking link** customers can open from Instagram, Google or WhatsApp, in Arabic and English.
- **Walk-ins and phone bookings** entered by reception into the same calendar, so there are no double bookings.
- **Multiple branches** under one dashboard, with each branch's staff and prices.

Staff schedules are also where pay is decided. If your stylists or trainers earn a commission per service or per session, the system should calculate it from completed bookings, not from a list someone types at the end of the month.

## Memberships, packages and classes

Gyms and many salons sell time in advance: a monthly membership, a 10-session package, a bundle of treatments. These are the records that cause most arguments, so the system has to hold them clearly.

| Product | What the system must track |
|---|---|
| Monthly or yearly membership | Start and end date, freeze periods, renewal reminders |
| Session package | Sessions bought, used and left, expiry date |
| Group class | Capacity, waiting list, check-ins |
| Gift card or prepaid balance | Balance, who used it and when |

Ask how a freeze works, what happens to unused sessions at expiry, and whether the customer can see their remaining balance themselves. A customer who can check "4 sessions left" on their phone does not need to call reception to ask.

For gyms, check-in matters as much as booking. A QR code at the door or a quick search at reception should confirm that the membership is active before the customer walks in.

The owner also needs a few reports that a notebook cannot give: bookings and revenue per staff member, no-shows per week, memberships expiring this month, and package sessions sold but not yet used. Unused sessions are money you have already received for work you still owe, so you should always know that figure.

## WhatsApp reminders and no-shows

Reminders work best where your customers already read their messages, and for many salons and gyms that is WhatsApp. A good setup sends:

- A confirmation when the booking is made, with the time, the service and the branch location.
- A reminder before the appointment, with a link to confirm, cancel or move it.
- A renewal reminder before a membership or package expires.

When a customer cancels through the link, the slot opens again for someone else. Some businesses go further and let customers book directly in a WhatsApp chat. We explain how that works in our guide to [starting a WhatsApp chatbot for your business](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-for-business-egypt).

Automated WhatsApp messages need a business account set up for sending them, with the message wording approved in advance. Allow time for that before launch.

## Payments and VAT invoices

Payments and invoices are where a booking system meets your accounts, so they deserve as much attention as the calendar.

### Payments: mada, cards and instalments

In Saudi Arabia, many customers pay with mada cards and digital wallets, and many also use buy-now-pay-later services such as Tabby and Tamara. Other Gulf countries have their own local rails, such as KNET in Kuwait and BenefitPay in Bahrain.

A booking system does not process cards itself. It connects to a licensed payment gateway, and the gateway handles the card or wallet. What the system must do is record each payment against the right booking or membership, handle deposits for long appointments, and manage refunds when a booking is cancelled.

Decide early which payments you want online and which you take at the counter. A deposit when booking is a common way to reduce no-shows for long or expensive services.

### VAT and e-invoicing

VAT applies to salon and gym sales in most of the Gulf: 15% in Saudi Arabia, 5% in the UAE and Oman, and 10% in Bahrain. Qatar and Kuwait have no VAT.

In Saudi Arabia, e-invoicing is already live. Since 2023 ZATCA has moved VAT-registered businesses onto Phase 2 in waves. Wave 25, covering VAT-able revenue above SAR 187,500 in any year from 2022 to 2025, must integrate with FATOORA by 1 Feb 2027. Sales to individual customers use simplified invoices, which must be reported to ZATCA within 24 hours. In practice, once your wave's deadline arrives, the invoices your booking or cashier system issues have to come from a solution that is connected to FATOORA.

In the UAE, e-invoicing becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more, through an accredited service provider, and B2C invoices are excluded for now. In Oman, e-invoicing becomes mandatory on 1 April 2027 for businesses above OMR 5 million and on 1 October 2027 for others, and B2C is included.

This is general information, not tax advice. Check your own obligations with your tax adviser. For the details country by country, see our guide to [e-invoicing across the GCC](/blog/gcc-e-invoicing-by-country).

## Build your own or subscribe to a ready-made system

Ready-made booking platforms are quick to start and cost a monthly fee. They suit a single branch with standard services. A custom system costs more up front but fits the way you actually work.

| Question | Ready-made subscription | Custom system |
|---|---|---|
| Time to start | Days | Weeks |
| Fits your services and packages | Only if they match the platform | Built around them |
| Arabic and English | Varies by platform | Designed for both |
| Your own brand and booking link | Usually the platform's | Your name and domain |
| Customer data | Held on the platform | In your own database |
| Cost over time | Monthly fee per branch or staff member | Build cost, then hosting and support |

A subscription is often the right first step. Custom starts to pay off when you have several branches, unusual packages, your own commission rules, or when you want bookings, memberships, the cashier and reports in one place instead of three tools. ## Common questions

### Can customers book without downloading an app?

Yes. A web booking link works in any phone browser and can be shared on Instagram, Google and WhatsApp. A separate app makes sense mainly for gyms with frequent check-ins and class bookings.

### Can one system handle both a salon and a gym?

Yes, if it is built around services, staff and packages rather than one type of business. Many spas and fitness centres sell both appointments and memberships.

### Do I need e-invoicing for a small salon in Saudi Arabia?

If your business is VAT-registered, your invoices fall under ZATCA's e-invoicing rules, and Phase 2 integration follows the wave ZATCA assigns you. Check your wave and obligations with your tax adviser.

### Can I move my existing customers and packages into a new system?

Usually, yes. Customer lists, active memberships and remaining sessions can be imported from spreadsheets or exports from your current platform. Check the import before launch, package by package.

### Does the system work for several branches?

It should. Each branch keeps its own staff, hours and prices, while you see bookings, revenue and memberships for all branches in one dashboard.
