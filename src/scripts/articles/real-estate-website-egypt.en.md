A real estate developer's website in Egypt has one job: turn a buyer's interest in a project into a sales conversation about a specific unit. That takes unit pages that stay current, galleries that load quickly on a phone, a payment-plan calculator that answers the instalment question, and every enquiry sent straight into your CRM with the unit and the ad that produced it.

## What the website is for, and what it is not

Buyers usually meet a project in an ad, on a property portal or through a broker. They come to your website to check that the developer is credible, see what is available and work out whether they can afford it. The website should answer those questions, then make contacting sales effortless.

It is not where your sales team works. Assignment, follow-up, reservations and contracts belong in a [real estate CRM](/blog/real-estate-crm-egypt); the website feeds it.

| Aspect | Website | CRM | Property portals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who uses it | Buyers and brokers | Your sales team | Buyers comparing developers |
| Main job | Show units, capture enquiries | Follow every lead to a contract | Reach buyers who do not know you yet |
| Who controls it | You | You | The portal |

## Projects and unit listings

Structure the site the way buyers think: project, then unit type, then unit. Each project page needs the location, unit types with areas, the delivery date, the payment plans on offer and the current phase. Each unit page needs the area, the floor plan, the floor or view where it matters, a starting price or range, and its status.

Status is where most sites go wrong. A buyer who enquires about a unit that sold last month starts the sales call disappointed. Two ways to avoid it:

- **Managed in the control panel:** the sales admin marks units available, reserved or sold, and the website updates immediately.
- **Read from the CRM:** the website shows the same status the sales team sees, so nothing is updated twice.

Filters should match how buyers search: project, unit type, bedrooms, area, budget and delivery year. Offer only the filters your inventory actually varies by.

## Galleries, floor plans and location

Photos and renders sell the project, and they are also what makes real estate sites slow. A good gallery shows a few large images first and loads the rest as the buyer swipes. Label renders as renders and date your construction photos.

Floor plans should open full screen and stay readable on a phone, with dimensions. Brochures help brokers, but the key facts should also be on the page, where Google can read them and buyers do not have to download a large PDF on mobile data.

For location, show a map with the distances buyers ask about, such as the main road, schools or the coast. A sales centre should have its own [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-maps-business-profile-egypt) so Google Maps leads buyers to the right door.

## A payment-plan calculator

Many developers in Egypt sell off-plan units on instalment plans, and for many buyers the monthly figure matters more than the total. A calculator lets them work it out alone, late at night, without waiting for a call back.

It takes the unit and its price, the plan (down payment percentage, number of years, monthly or quarterly instalments) and any separate payments such as a maintenance deposit. It then shows the down payment, each instalment and the schedule. Two rules keep it honest:

1. Plans are set by your sales team in the control panel, not hard-coded, so a changed plan reaches the calculator the same day.
2. The result is labelled as an estimate, with the final schedule confirmed in the reservation contract.

The calculator is also a natural place to capture a lead: offer to send the exact schedule by WhatsApp in exchange for a name and phone number.

## Lead capture straight into the CRM

Every enquiry should reach your CRM within seconds with four things attached: who the buyer is, which unit they looked at, how they want to be contacted and which campaign brought them.

- **Short forms.** Name, phone number and preferred contact time are enough; every extra field costs enquiries.
- **WhatsApp with context.** The button opens a chat with a prefilled message naming the project and unit.
- **Campaign tracking.** The site keeps the ad source from the first visit and sends it with the lead, so you know which ads produce sales, not just clicks.
- **No inbox in between.** Forms that email a shared address get lost on launch days; send them to the CRM through an integration.

For a launch, send ad traffic to a [landing page](/blog/landing-page-for-ads-egypt) built for that project, feeding the same CRM. Because these forms collect personal data, check what [Egypt's data protection law](/blog/egypt-data-protection-law-apps) expects on consent and retention.

## Fast on phones, in Arabic and English

Many buyers open your site from an ad on their phone, often on mobile data, and Google uses the mobile version of a site for indexing and ranking. Its Core Web Vitals set the targets: the main content should load within 2.5 seconds, the page should respond to a tap within 200 milliseconds, and the layout should barely shift (a score of 0.1 or less). Real estate sites usually miss the first because of hero videos and full-size renders. The fixes are routine:

- images resized for each screen and served as WebP or AVIF;
- a poster image in the hero, with video playing only on tap;
- 3D tours and maps loaded only when the buyer opens them.

Arabic and English should be two complete versions with their own pages, a proper right-to-left layout and their own page titles, because Arabic-speaking buyers search in Arabic.

## Common questions

### Do we need our own website if we already list on property portals?

Yes. Portals are good for reach, but they show your units beside your competitors' and you control little of the page. Your own site is where buyers check the developer and where enquiries arrive with the unit attached.

### Should we show prices on the website?

A starting price per unit type, plus the payment plans, filters out buyers who cannot afford the project and makes a calculator possible. Manage prices from the control panel so the site is never out of date.

### Can the website show which units are still available?

Yes, from the control panel or read directly from your CRM. The CRM route is better, because sales updates the status once and the website follows.

### Do we need a mobile app as well?

Usually not for selling: buyers come through ads and search, and a fast website serves them without an install. An app makes more sense later, for owners after handover.
