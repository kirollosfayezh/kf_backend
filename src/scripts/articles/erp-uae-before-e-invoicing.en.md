If you are choosing an ERP system in the UAE now, make e-invoicing readiness a selection criterion, not a later add-on: from 2027 your B2B and B2G invoices must leave your system as structured PINT AE data sent through an Accredited Service Provider (ASP). The right ERP is the one that produces clean invoice data, connects to the ASP you choose, applies 5% VAT correctly across your entities, and costs what you expected over its whole life. Whether that is a packaged product, a custom build or a hybrid depends on how unusual your operations are.

Last checked: 6 November 2026

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Confirm your own obligations with your tax adviser.

## What e-invoicing changes about choosing an ERP

Under Ministerial Decisions 243 and 244 of 2025, the UAE is moving all B2B and B2G transactions to e-invoicing. Both the business issuing the invoice and the business receiving it must appoint an ASP. B2C sales are excluded until the Minister decides otherwise.

The model has five "corners". Your ERP (corner 1) sends PINT AE data to your ASP (corner 2). Your ASP validates it and passes it to the buyer's ASP (corner 3), which delivers it to the buyer (corner 4). Your ASP also reports the tax data to the tax side (corner 5). A PDF, a Word file, a scan or an email is not an e-invoice.

The deadlines decide how much time you have to choose:

| Group | Appoint an ASP by | Go live |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue of AED 50m or more | 30 October 2026 | 1 January 2027 |
| Revenue below AED 50m | 31 March 2027 | 1 July 2027 |
| Government entities | 31 March 2027 | 1 October 2027 |

Revenue means gross income in your most recent accounting period. Pilot and voluntary adopters could start from 1 July 2026. For the full checklist by group, see [UAE e-invoicing deadlines for SMEs](/blog/uae-e-invoicing-deadlines-smes).

## E-invoicing readiness: what to test before you sign

Every vendor will say "e-invoicing ready". Ask them to show it:

- **A real PINT AE export.** Ask for the output of a normal invoice, a credit note and an invoice with mixed VAT treatment, and have your shortlisted ASP check that it accepts them.
- **The ASP connection.** Which ASPs does the ERP already connect to, and how? With 86 accredited ASPs on the Ministry of Finance list, you want to choose the ASP on service and price, not because it is the only one your ERP supports.
- **Incoming invoices.** You will also receive e-invoices from suppliers. Ask how they arrive in the ERP and whether they can be matched to purchase orders and goods received without retyping.
- **Rejections and corrections.** What the user sees when the ASP rejects an invoice, and how a credit note is linked back to the original.
- **Master data.** Customer and supplier records, item descriptions and VAT codes must be complete and consistent. The ERP should make missing data hard to save, not discover it at send time.
- **Who maintains it.** The specification can change. Ask who updates the connector, how quickly, and whether that is in the subscription or billed separately.
- **Audit trail.** Every invoice should show who created it, what was sent, when, and what the ASP replied.

Onboarding itself means choosing an ASP, registering on EmaraTax and then connecting. The ERP does not replace the ASP: it feeds it. For the technical side, see [UAE e-invoicing for custom POS and ERP systems](/blog/uae-e-invoicing-custom-pos-erp).

The penalties make this concrete. Under Cabinet Decision 173 of 2026, announced on 30 October 2026, not implementing the system or not appointing an ASP costs AED 5,000, then AED 5,000 per month, up to AED 50,000 in any 12 months. Each e-invoice or credit note not issued or transmitted costs AED 100, up to AED 5,000 per month. Not telling your ASP about changed registration data costs AED 250 per day, up to AED 5,000 per month.

## VAT, free zones and multi-entity set-ups

E-invoicing sits on top of VAT, so the ERP must get VAT right first.

- **The standard rate is 5%.** The ERP should apply it by default and support other treatments line by line, so a single invoice can carry mixed items correctly.
- **Imported services.** When a non-resident supplier provides services to a VAT-registered UAE business, the supplier does not charge VAT and the UAE recipient self-accounts for it. Your ERP should record these purchases so the reverse charge appears in your VAT return.
- **Designated zones.** The FTA's guide says services supplied in designated zones are treated as supplied in the UAE and are liable to VAT at the standard rate. Do not configure a free-zone entity as "no VAT" by default.

Many UAE groups run more than one legal entity: a free-zone company, a mainland company, perhaps a branch. Free-zone companies operate under a different legal framework and, to sell on the mainland, need a licensed mainland distributor or a mainland branch or company. An ERP for that kind of group should handle:

- separate legal entities with their own invoice numbering, VAT settings and bank accounts;
- intercompany sales and transfers, with stock moving between entities on paper and in reality;
- consolidated reporting across entities, without exporting everything to a spreadsheet;
- tax groups, because Ministerial Decision 197 of 2026 excludes transactions inside a VAT tax group from e-invoicing for 24 months from 1 January 2027.

The same decision removed the requirement to store e-invoice data inside the UAE, which gives you more freedom in where the ERP is hosted. Sector rules still apply: payment service providers must keep personal and payment data in the UAE, and health data is restricted.

## Packaged, custom or hybrid

There are three realistic routes. None is right for everyone.

| Route | Best when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Packaged ERP | Your processes are close to standard accounting, sales and purchasing | Per-user fees that grow with you, and paying for customisation that fights the product |
| Custom ERP | Your operations are unusual: projects, rentals, production, field work, several entities working differently | Longer build, and you need a developer who documents the system and hands over the code |
| Hybrid | Accounting fits a packaged product, but operations do not | Two systems to keep in sync; agree which one owns each piece of data |

A hybrid is common: keep a packaged accounting product for the ledger and VAT returns, and build a custom operations system for inventory, orders, jobs or a branch POS that feeds it. In that case, decide early which system issues the e-invoice, because only one should.

A custom ERP is worth it when a packaged product would need so much configuration that you are effectively building software anyway. We explain how to weigh this in [custom software vs off-the-shelf: how to choose](/blog/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf-how-to-choose). If you are not sure you need a full ERP at all, [does a small business actually need an ERP?](/blog/erp-for-small-business-egypt) walks through the signs; it was written for Egypt, but the signs are the same in the UAE.

## The real cost of ownership

The licence or build price is only part of the cost. Compare options over three to five years:

| Cost line | Packaged ERP | Custom ERP |
|---|---|---|
| Licences or subscription | Per user or per module, every year | None, unless third-party services are used |
| Implementation | Configuration, training, data migration | Design, build, testing, data migration |
| E-invoicing | Connector, possibly an add-on fee, plus the ASP's own fees | Connector built into the system, plus the ASP's own fees |
| Changes later | Partner rates for customisation; some changes are not possible | Developer time; anything is possible, at a price |
| Hosting | Usually included in cloud subscriptions | Your server or cloud account |
| Exit | Data export from the vendor's format | You own the code and the database |

ASP fees apply whichever ERP you choose, so ask ASPs for their pricing at the same time as ERP vendors.

The most expensive mistake is usually data, not software. Messy item lists, duplicate customers and untrusted stock figures follow you into any new system, so clean them first: [inventory management: where your stock really goes](/blog/inventory-management-system-egypt) covers the common leaks.

## A selection plan that fits the deadline

If your revenue is below AED 50m, you have until 31 March 2027 to appoint an ASP and until 1 July 2027 to go live. Working back from that:

1. **Map your invoices.** List who you invoice, how many invoices and credit notes a month, and which entities issue them.
2. **Clean your master data.** Customers, suppliers, items and VAT codes, before any demo.
3. **Shortlist two or three ERP routes**, including a hybrid if operations are unusual.
4. **Shortlist ASPs in parallel**, from the Ministry of Finance's accredited list.
5. **Run a test invoice end to end** with your preferred ERP and ASP before signing.
6. **Migrate and train**, then run old and new systems side by side for at least one month-end close.
7. **Go live on e-invoicing** with time to spare before your deadline.

If you are in the AED 50m-plus group, the ASP deadline has already passed and go-live is 1 January 2027, so focus on step 5 now and treat a full ERP replacement as a separate, later project.

## Common questions

### Can we keep our current ERP and still meet e-invoicing?

Often yes, if it can export structured invoice data and connect to an ASP, directly or through a connector. If it cannot, you can extend it, replace it, or use your ASP's portal for low invoice volumes. Test it with a real invoice before deciding.

### Does the ERP vendor have to be an accredited ASP?

No. The ASP is the accredited provider that validates and exchanges your e-invoices. Your ERP produces the data and sends it to the ASP you appoint. Check the ASP against the Ministry of Finance's published list.

### Do B2C sales from our shop need e-invoices?

Not for now. B2C transactions are excluded until the Minister issues a decision. Your normal VAT invoicing obligations still apply, and it is sensible to choose a system that could produce structured data later.

### Must our ERP data be hosted in the UAE?

Not because of e-invoicing: Ministerial Decision 197 of 2026 removed the requirement to store e-invoice data in the UAE. Other rules can apply to specific data, such as payment or health data, so check your sector.

### Is a custom ERP slower to get ready than a packaged one?

It can be, which is why a hybrid often makes sense before a deadline. Use a packaged product or an existing system for compliant invoicing now, and build the custom operations side in stages.
