## Qatar rules we design for

### Payments: Himyan, NAPS, QPay and Fawran

Himyan is Qatar's national card: the debit version launched on 30 March 2024 and runs on NAPS, and online shopping goes through the QPay portal ([QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/news/news-details?id=0019-qatar-central-bank-launches-%27himyan%27-debit-card&date=30%2F03%2F2024)). Qatar Central Bank planned to launch Fawran, a 24/7 instant transfer service, in March 2024 ([QNA](https://www.qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2024-02/19/0030-qcb-to-launch-instant-payment-service-%27fawran%27-in-march)).

A store or app does not connect to these networks directly: it connects to a payment gateway or acquiring bank, which decides the methods your customers see. Ask it about Himyan cards, fees, refunds and settlement before the checkout is designed; we then connect it through its published API. Shopify Payments is not available in Qatar, so a Shopify store there needs a third-party payment provider ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries)).

### E-invoicing: a draft law, no date yet

Qatar has no VAT; excise tax has applied since 1 January 2019 ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/other-taxes)). On 6 May 2026 the Cabinet approved a draft e-invoicing law and its executive regulations, prepared by the Ministry of Finance with the General Tax Authority ([QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/news/news-details?id=cabinet-holds-regular-meeting&date=6%2F05%2F2026)). EY expects it to apply from 1 January 2027 ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1020-qatar-approves-draft-e-invoicing-law-and-implementing-regulations)), but we found no official start date or technical specification when we checked on 25 November 2026.

What this means today: don't pay for "Qatar e-invoicing compliance", because there are no published technical rules to comply with yet. We keep your orders and invoices as structured data in the database, not only as PDFs, so connecting them to the final system is a contained piece of work. See [e-invoicing in the GCC: where each country stands](/blog/gcc-e-invoicing-by-country).

### Personal data: Law 13 of 2016

Law 13 of 2016 is overseen by the NCGAA, part of the National Cyber Security Agency. Breaches must be reported within 72 hours, sensitive data needs authorisation and a data protection impact assessment, fines reach QAR 5 million, and the Qatar Financial Centre has its own regime ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/countries/qatar/law.html)).

We build access logs and alerts for the 72-hour notice, ask forms for only the data an order or booking needs, and tell you in writing where the database and backups are hosted. This is general information, not legal advice: for a clinic, a financial service or a company in the Qatar Financial Centre, check the rules with a lawyer in Qatar. More in [website and app projects in Qatar: what's different](/blog/website-app-development-qatar).

### Digital identity

Tawtheeq, Qatar's National Authentication System run by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, gives users one login across government e-services ([MCIT](https://www.mcit.gov.qa/en/services/national-authentication-service)). The ministry's national digital authentication and trust services strategy aims to extend that collaboration to private-sector organisations ([Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/698369/qatar/mcit-launches-national-digital-authentication-and-trust-services-strategy)). If your service needs to verify who a user is, confirm with the ministry what a private service can use before the feature is planned.

### Delivery apps

Keeta has operated in Doha since 19 August 2025 ([imsilkroad.com](https://en.imsilkroad.com/p/347199.html)), and Snoonu is majority-owned by Saudi Arabia's Jahez, which completed a 76.56% stake in October 2025 ([Bridge MENA](https://bridgemena.com/news/jahez-completes-second-phase-of-acquisition-in-snoonu)). ## How we work with you from Cairo

### The same working week

Qatar's weekend is Friday and Saturday ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/mena/2021/12/07/when-is-the-weekend-in-the-arab-world)). We work Sunday to Thursday, 10:00 to 18:00 Cairo time, so our working days are yours.

### Doha time

Doha is on UTC+3 all year. Cairo is on the same time from the last Friday of April to the last Thursday of October and one hour behind for the rest of the year ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContentP/1/566547/Egypt/Egypt-to-move-clocks-forward-one-hour-on-Friday-fo.aspx); offsets from the [IANA time zone database](https://www.iana.org/time-zones)).

## Common questions

### Do you have an office in Qatar?

No. We are based in Cairo and work with companies in Qatar remotely, in the same working week, Sunday to Thursday. The first step is a free 30-minute WhatsApp call, booked in your own time zone.

### Is e-invoicing mandatory in Qatar?

Not yet. The Cabinet approved a draft law on 6 May 2026, and we found no official start date or technical specification as of 25 November 2026. Keep invoices as structured data so you can adapt when the rules are final.

### Which payment methods should our store accept?

Himyan cards on NAPS, used online through the QPay portal, alongside international cards. The payment gateway you contract with decides how each method is connected, so choose it before the checkout is designed.

### Do you work with companies in the Qatar Financial Centre?

Yes. The way we build is the same; what changes is the data protection regime, because the Qatar Financial Centre has its own. We plan consent, storage and access rules to the regime that applies to you.

### What are your working hours in Doha time?

Sunday to Thursday, 10:00 to 18:00 from the last Friday of April to the last Thursday of October, and 11:00 to 19:00 for the rest of the year.

### Who owns the code and the app-store accounts?

Your company does. The code, the database, and the hosting, domain and developer accounts are all in its name, and apps are published under your own Apple and Google accounts.
